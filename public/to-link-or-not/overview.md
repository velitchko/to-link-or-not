# Study Overview

Welcome, and thank you for participating!

## What This Study Is About

Network diagrams (nodes connected by links) are used everywhere — from social networks to knowledge graphs to biological pathways. But drawing all connections at once can make dense graphs hard to read.

This study evaluates **four strategies** for showing (or hiding) connections in node-link diagrams. We want to understand which strategy helps people answer network-analysis questions most accurately and efficiently.

## Structure of the Study

The study consists of **36 trials** grouped into four blocks. In each block you will use a different link-rendering style. Before each block, a short tutorial explains the style you are about to use.

Each trial shows a network diagram and asks one of three question types:

| Task | Question |
|------|----------|
| **T1 – Hub identification** | Which node has the most connections? |
| **T2 – Common neighbors** | Which nodes are connected to *both* highlighted nodes? |
| **T3 – Community detection** | Select all nodes that form a distinct cluster. |

## What To Expect

- Each trial has no time limit — take as long as you need.
- Click nodes to select your answer, then press **Submit Answer**.
- You will get feedback during the short training tasks so you can learn the interaction.
- During the real study trials, you will **not** receive correctness feedback; after submitting, click **Next** to continue.
- The entire session takes about **30–45 minutes**.

## A Few Reminders

- Please complete the study in a single sitting in a quiet environment.
- Use a desktop or laptop browser (not a mobile device).
- If anything is unclear, click the **?** button at any time.

Click **Next** when you are ready to begin.
